# Mainbus icon concepts

## The app icon in use

The app's own icon is drawn separately from the concepts below. Its sources are here:

- `mainbus.svg`: the full-colour app icon.
- `mainbus-tray-black.svg` and `mainbus-tray-white.svg`: the monochrome tray glyph, for light and dark taskbars.

What's built from them:

- `pnpm icons:build` (`scripts/icons-build.mjs`) renders the PNGs the running app loads into `resources/icons/`, and that output is committed:
  - a 256 px window, taskbar and notification icon;
  - the tray glyph at 16, 20, 24, 32, 40 and 48 px.
- electron-builder copies `resources/icons/` into the packaged app.
- The packager's own files stay in `build/` and are not shipped: `icon.ico` for the exe, `icon.icns` and the 1024 px `icon.png`. Re-export them by hand when the icon changes.
- The renderer's favicon is `src/renderer/public/favicon.ico`.

## The #110 concepts

Seven concepts for the Mainbus application icon, from #110. They were not adopted: the custom icon above was used instead.

- **Contact sheet:** [`preview.png`](preview.png). Every concept at 256, 64, 32 and 16 px, then the 16 px rendering blown up 4× so you can see the real pixels. Each is shown on Windows 11's light (`#F3F3F3`) and dark (`#202020`) taskbar colours, and as a Night Owl variant on Night Owl's ground.
- **Taskbar and tray mock-ups:** [`taskbar.png`](taskbar.png). Each concept at 16 px in a taskbar button and in the tray, light and dark, without and with the orange "needs you" dot. The dot has the tray's current geometry (`src/main/tray/icons.ts`: centre at 72%, radius 26%).
- **Regenerate both:** `pnpm icons:preview` (`scripts/icons-preview.mjs`). It uses the repo's Playwright to drive the repo's own Electron, so there is nothing new to download.

## Files

Each concept is `NN-name.svg` on a 256×256 viewBox. Where the full design breaks up at small sizes, `NN-name-16.svg` is a simplified, pixel-aligned version on a 16×16 grid; the sheets use it at 32 and 16 px.

All of them use the app's palette (`src/renderer/src/theme.ts`): a Working blue `#1E3F99` tile, a `#F4F4F1` ground-coloured mark, and lighter tints of the blue (`#3A5FC4`, `#7E9BE6`, `#A9BEF2`) for secondary parts. They have no text and no gradients. None of them uses orange, so the "needs you" dot keeps orange to itself. The Night Owl variant is generated by the preview script, which swaps the colours: tile `#0B2942`, mark `#82AAFF`, secondary parts `#1D3B53` and `#5F7E97`.

Every mark keeps its important parts in the top and left of the tile. At the tray's current dot size, the dot still covers about a quarter of a 16 px icon. For the adoption ticket, a slightly smaller dot (about 20% radius), or one with a 1 px ring in the taskbar colour, would separate better from all of them.

## The concepts

### 01 Main bus (`01-main-bus.svg`, `01-main-bus-16.svg`)
**Idea:** the name's origin, drawn abstractly: one thick line across the top, with branches dropping off it to feed "machines". The branches get shorter to the right, which leaves the bottom right free for the dot.
- **Strengths:** it tells the name's story; the shape is unusual and carries no letter or brand; the dot's corner is naturally empty.
- **At small sizes:** the 16 px variant keeps the bar and two fed blocks and reads as a clear "T with blocks". At 16 px the story is gone and it is just a pleasant shape. Without its blocks it could read as a "T" or "π".

### 02 Board (`02-board.svg`, `02-board-16.svg`)
**Idea:** three lanes with cards in them, and one card tilted between lanes as it moves.
- **Strengths:** the most literal picture of what the app is (a ticket board); calm and orderly.
- **At small sizes:** the 16 px variant is a neat grid of white blocks on lighter lanes and holds up well. But at 16 px it looks much like any kanban or "grid/apps" icon (Trello-like boards are a common genre), so it is less distinctive.

### 03 Lineup (`03-lineup.svg`)
**Idea:** three linked nodes rising from bottom left to top right: Planner → Implementer → Reviewer, the relay a ticket runs.
- **Strengths:** a very simple, bold geometry that survives at 16 px with no variant. The rising line suggests progress.
- **At small sizes:** it is crisp. However, three linked dots are close to the generic "share" or "connection" glyphs, and the middle node sits near the dot.

### 04 Hub (`04-hub.svg`, `04-hub-16.svg`)
**Idea:** a central orchestrator with spokes to its agents. The gap at the bottom right is where the "needs you" dot lands, as if it were one more agent.
- **Strengths:** it shows the orchestrator idea directly; the dot fits it meaningfully.
- **At small sizes:** the 16 px variant cuts it to four satellites and is still recognisable. But it is busy at 32 px, and hub-and-spoke reads as "molecule" or "network", which is common.

### 05 Monogram (`05-monogram.svg`, `05-monogram-16.svg`)
**Idea:** a geometric "M" drawn as a continuous track, with a groove down its middle that makes it read as a lane. The right leg stops short, to leave room for the dot.
- **Strengths:** the most legible at 16 px of all; instantly "M" for Mainbus. Its short right leg is a small, ownable quirk.
- **At small sizes:** the groove disappears, so the 16 px variant is a plain bold M. On the other hand, a bare "M" is the least distinctive idea: many products use an M, though this one does not copy any of their forms.

### 06 Ticket (`06-ticket.svg`, `06-ticket-16.svg`)
**Idea:** a tilted admission-style ticket with a perforation, the unit of work Mainbus runs.
- **Strengths:** friendly and distinctive at large sizes; on theme ("tickets").
- **At small sizes:** the weakest. The tilt and details turn to mush, so the 16 px variant is a straight ticket with notches, which can read as a bow-tie or a film frame. The dot also lands on the ticket's corner.

### 07 Converge (`07-converge.svg`)
**Idea:** two side lanes curve into one thick main line that runs on to the right: parallel work merging into main.
- **Strengths:** simple, bold and calm; it reads at 16 px with no variant. The lower half, and so the dot's corner, is almost empty.
- **At small sizes:** it is crisp, though it can read as an arrow or the "merge" glyph from developer tools, which is generic rather than any one brand.

## Originality

The concepts are built only from rounded rectangles, circles and strokes in the app's own blues. None of them uses Anthropic's or Claude's marks (no starburst, no asterisk-like radial shape, no terracotta) or Factorio's look (no gears, belts, industrial textures or orange-grey palette); the main bus is an abstract bar-and-branch diagram. None of them copies another well-known app icon's form.

## My top two

1. **01 Main bus.** It is the only concept that carries the name's own story, its silhouette is unlike any common app icon, and it was designed around the dot's corner. With the 16 px variant it stays a clean, recognisable shape in the tray.
2. **07 Converge.** It is the boldest and cleanest at 16 px without needing a variant, fits "parallel agents, one main line" well, and leaves the dot's corner empty. It would also make the simplest favicon for mainbus.app.
